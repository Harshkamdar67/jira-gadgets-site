When each one fits

Use what Jira already has if…

  • You look at one project's epics at a time. The timeline shows a progress bar on every epic once Progress is turned on in View settings.
  • Your team runs Scrum on a company-managed board and estimates consistently. Epic burndown already forecasts the sprint an epic will finish in.
  • You're on Jira Premium. Plans covers cross-project roadmaps, capacity and release estimates.

Choose ESU Lite if…

  • You want time totals (original, remaining and spent) on the epic itself, where people already look.
  • You're on Data Center as well as Cloud.
  • You may later want ESU Standard's extras, such as editing values in a worksheet, time budgets or containers.

Choose Epic Progress if…

  • You want many epics on one dashboard or wallboard, picked by project or JQL across projects.
  • You estimate in story points, or work in Kanban, or use team-managed projects, where Jira's epic reports don't apply.
  • You want a finish date with a confidence level, and a nudge when an epic is late, stuck or growing.

Epic Progress limits, plainly

  • It's a dashboard gadget only. It adds nothing to the epic's own page.
  • It reads Jira and never changes it, so there's no editing or bulk actions.
  • Up to 50 epics and 3,000 child issues per gadget.
  • Forecasts count finished issues, not points or hours, and need two weeks of history and three finished issues. See how forecasts work.
  • Time is measured against the original estimate. Remaining estimate isn't shown.
  • Jira Cloud only. There's no Data Center version.
