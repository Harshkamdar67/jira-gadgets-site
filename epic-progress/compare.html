Which to use

Jira's own tools are enough if you look at one project at a time. Turn on Progress in the timeline's View settings and each epic gets a bar. If your team runs Scrum on a company-managed board and estimates most of its work, Epic burndown already predicts the sprint an epic will finish in. On Premium, Plans covers roadmaps across projects.

ESU Lite puts time totals (original, remaining and spent) on the epic itself. It also runs on Data Center. Its paid Standard edition adds a dashboard gadget, story points, a worksheet for editing values, and time budgets.

Epic Progress is for a dashboard or wallboard that shows many epics at once, chosen by project or by JQL across projects. It can measure by story points or time on any board, including Kanban boards and team-managed projects, where Jira's epic reports aren't available. Each epic also gets a forecast finish date and a flag when, for example, the forecast lands after the due date or nothing has been finished in two weeks.

Epic Progress limits

  • It's a dashboard gadget only. It adds nothing to the epic's own page.
  • It reads Jira and never changes it, so there's no editing or bulk actions.
  • Only the first 15 open issues are listed under each epic. A link opens a Jira search for the rest.
  • Up to 50 epics and 3,000 child issues per gadget.
  • Forecasts count finished issues, not points or hours, and need two weeks of history and three finished issues. See how forecasts work.
  • Time is measured against the original estimate. Remaining estimate isn't shown.
  • Jira Cloud only. There's no Data Center version.
