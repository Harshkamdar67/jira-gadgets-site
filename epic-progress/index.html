Epic Progress

See how far along every epic is, when it will likely finish, and which ones need attention, on any Jira Cloud dashboard.

Add it to a dashboard

  1. Open a dashboard and select Edit.
  2. Select Add gadget, search for Epic Progress, then select Add.
  3. Choose which epics to show and how to measure progress, then select Save.

To change the settings later, edit the dashboard, open the gadget's ⋯ menu and select Configure.

Settings

SettingWhat it does
Which epicsAll open epics in a project, or epics returned by your own JQL query. JQL is checked when you save, and Jira's error is shown if it can't be used.
Measure progress byIssues (done of total), Story points (points done of points estimated) or Time (hours logged of hours estimated, including sub-tasks).
SortLeast complete first, most work remaining, due date, or backlog order.
Show up to1 to 50 epics.
Include completed epicsAlso show epics whose status is done.
Show completion forecastsShow the forecast line under each epic.

Forecasts

Each epic shows a line such as Likely done Nov 14 · 85% sure by Nov 27. The forecast runs 1,000 simulations based on how many of the epic's issues were finished each week, so it reflects how the team actually works rather than estimates.

  • Only weeks since work on the epic began are used, up to the last eight weeks.
  • A forecast appears after two weeks of history and at least three finished issues. Until then the gadget says so instead of guessing.
  • Forecasts count issues, whichever measure you show.

Risk flags

FlagMeaning
At riskThe cautious (85%) forecast lands after the epic's due date.
OverdueThe due date has passed and work remains.
StalledNothing in the epic has been finished in two weeks.
Scope growingMore issues were added than finished in the last two weeks.

Good to know

  • Works in company-managed and team-managed projects. Child issues are found through the Parent field.
  • Story points are read from either "Story Points" or "Story point estimate", whichever your site uses.
  • Everyone sees only epics and issues they already have permission to view.
  • Up to 3,000 child issues are counted per gadget. A note appears if that limit is reached.

Your data

Epic Progress only reads Jira data to draw the gadget. It stores nothing of its own beyond the gadget settings Jira keeps for every dashboard, and no data leaves Atlassian. See the privacy policy.
